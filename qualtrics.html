QUALTRICS

Head of UX Design & Research

At Qualtrics I led the global UX Design & Research organization, responsible for great user and buyer experiences across all Qualtrics products. I built the team and its disciplines from the ground up to 20+ people across UX design, visual design, user research, and information architecture, for web and mobile.

I was in the boardroom when Qualtrics decided to go after Experience Management (XM) as its category, as one of about a dozen key executives who voted on whether to move forward. I designed the XM brand concept from scratch, and personally designed the UX of the merged products in the new XM platform, unifying 7 separate products into one. That platform helped make Qualtrics an attractive acquisition target and prepare to go public, before SAP acquired it for $8B in 2018.

The XM brand concept I designed from scratch: four dots for the four pillars of Experience Management (employee, product, brand, and customer experience).

The Challenge

Seven Products, One Platform

When I joined Qualtrics, it had a range of very different web applications, each with its own user experience and technology stack. One of my main missions was to bring them together into one platform.

Before: separate applications, each with its own experience.

I personally designed the UX of the merged products in the Experience Management (XM) Platform, and my team and I brought the applications together with a common design system covering interaction, workflow, and visual design.

After: one XM Platform with a shared design system.

Research & Workflows

Designing for End-to-End Workflows

Research uncovered many complex workflows across Qualtrics and third-party products, which we then improved through product design. We used mini-wires to communicate them. This example shows the customer experience (CX) workflows, including the operational and strategic flows we focused on adding and differentiating with.

Upsell and Third-Party Integrations

The XM Platform had many third-party extensions to integrate and automate customer experience data sources and outputs. This design separates standard, included functionality from optional add-ons. The key was to make upsell something users saw value in, and could dismiss if they weren't interested.

Innovation

A Patented Sentiment Visualization

Creating competitive advantage through patents was a key focus. One example: I came up with this new data visualization for our AI-powered sentiment analysis tool. It shows several dimensions at once: overall topic and sentiment (the color of the core circle), prevalence (the size of the circle), and how responses within that topic are distributed by sentiment. We patented it (US 20220138265).

Measuring Success

Continuous Usability Benchmarking

My team and I established the first continuous usability benchmarking program at Qualtrics: an in-product SUS survey across all products, sent to cohorts of users at a set frequency. We reported the results continuously on dashboards in our office, open to all of Qualtrics, so we could compare Qualtrics products with each other on usability and usefulness, and with the wider tech industry. Once set up, it ran automatically with minimal maintenance.
